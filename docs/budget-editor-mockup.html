Every approved revision is kept. The spend report always reads the latest approved figures, so a historical report re-run months later shows the budget that was live at the time.

Decisions baked into this mockup — confirm or change:
  1. One revision covers one segment for the whole year. All of Marketing's cat_l1/cat_l2 lines × 12 months are approved or rejected as a unit. A BO working on Marketing and Factory raises two revisions.
  2. Approved figures stay live while a draft is edited. The spend report never reads a draft. Editing budget can't accidentally change what the report shows.
  3. Every revision is retained, not overwritten. Needed for the audit trail, and it means a report for a past period can show the budget that was actually live then — say if that matters, because it changes the schema.
  4. No self-approval. A user holding both BO and CEO can submit but not approve their own revision. Same control as the KinOS write-off rule.
  5. Rejection requires a note, and it returns the revision to draft with the BO's figures intact so they can amend rather than retype.
  6. Entry ergonomics carry this feature. 5 lines × 12 months is 60 cells here; a real segment may be 15 lines and 180 cells. Paste-a-row, fill-right, copy-last-year and arrow-key navigation are not polish — without them nobody will keep the budget current.
  7. cat_l2 lines come from the existing category list, so budget can only be set against categories that requests can actually be filed under. Adding a line means adding a category in Settings first.
