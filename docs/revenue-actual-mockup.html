How to read a cell — Budget is the approved plan for that month, Actual is what was spent. Used is the share of budget spent — the cell tint follows it. Of rev is that spend as a share of the month's actual revenue.

Click a segment to drill to category, then again for sub-category. Tint follows budget use — green under 80%, amber to 100%, red over. September's budget is pro-rated to 23 days, so a part month is judged fairly.

What this adds:
  1. Goal and actual sit in one cell. The goal is the primary figure; the actual is beneath it, green when above goal and red when below. One row per channel, not two trees to compare by eye.
  2. Two different percentages, deliberately. On the budget page a budget line shows its share of the revenue goal — that is planning. On the spend report, actual spend shows its share of actual revenue — that is what happened.
  3. Every month cell shows both figures, not a percentage alone. Budget on top in grey, actual spend beneath in bold, percentages last. The revenue row reads identically — goal then actual — so one scanning habit works for the whole table.
  4. Month view is the other way to read it. Switching the report to Month already gives a single month with Budget, Actual, Pending and variance as full-width columns — that exists today. Year view is for spotting which month went wrong; Month view is for reading one month closely.
  5. The current month is marked partial on both sides. Comparing a full month's goal against a part-month actual would read as a miss when it is just an incomplete month.
  6. Actuals are synced, never typed. Goals stay hand-entered by CEO or admin; actuals come from the revenue sheet on a schedule, with a "Sync now" button and a visible last-synced time.
  7. Months with no actual yet show an em dash, never zero.
Needed before the sync can be built:
  1. A REVENUE_SYNC tab in the sheet with plain columns — bu, category, sub_category, channel, year, month, actual — filled by formulas pointing at the existing blocks. Reading the presentation layout directly would break the first time someone inserts a column.
  2. The channel names in the sheet, so they can be matched to the 21 channels in the portal. Song Wat, Talat Noi, Siam Discovery and the rest must line up exactly, or actuals land against nothing.
  3. Where SV's revenue lives — the file we looked at was ONEST only.
  4. A decision on what happens when the sheet holds a channel the portal does not: reject the sync, or import it and flag it for review.
